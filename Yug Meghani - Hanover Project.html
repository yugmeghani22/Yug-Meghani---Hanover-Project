Operating Principles

Four things I'd hold myself to.

Posture before activities. The way I'd walk in on Day 1, and check myself against on Day 30.

01

Ship the controls before the wires.

At 25 payments a day, I can't manually re-verify every wire — and I can't tolerate a single bad one. So the job is to design the controls that catch errors, not just run the queue. Every wire I touch is a chance to find the next check that should exist.

02

Every near-miss is roadmap input.

An almost-error caught is more valuable than a clean wire shipped. I'd keep a structured near-miss log from Day 1 — every variance, every soft-flag, every "we caught it just in time" moment. By Day 30, that log becomes the automation memo.

03

AI prepares — I review.

Not the other way around. The platform does the heavy lift; my job is to be the human judgment layer Hanover Park sells to GPs. That means pattern recognition, edge-case handling, and trust calibration — not data entry, not box-checking.

04

Automate myself out, not up.

The role description says it twice: I should be eliminating the manual parts of my own job. That's not threatening — it's the only way the seat scales as Hanover Park grows. Every checklist I write is one a teammate (or the platform) can run later.

The 30-Day Arc

Four weeks. Each one ends in a deliverable.

Aggressive but honest. Fully owning everything by Day 10 reads naive. Shadowing for 30 days reads low-agency. The middle path is the right signal.

01
DAYS 01–07
PHASE · LEARN

Establish the baseline.

Understand what 25 payments/day looks like across the whole system — and where errors enter today before AI catches them.

  • Shadow each of the 6 payment types end-to-end. Walk all 7 stations for each.
  • Build the payment taxonomy — trigger, source-of-truth, AI-prep boundary, human-review boundary, failure modes.
  • Sit in on 3 client escalations or near-miss reviews — the most concentrated learning per minute.
  • Get fluent in the platform as a user — task-level, not concept-level.
End of Week 1
Payment Taxonomy v1

All 6 types mapped, plus a one-page "where errors enter" memo.

02
DAYS 08–14
PHASE · OWN

Take the wheel.

Move from shadowing to processing. Start with high-volume / low-stakes, work up to LP-facing wires by EOW.

  • Take primary ownership of bill pay + intercompany (~10 of the 25 daily payments).
  • Co-process capital calls + distributions with a senior reviewer. Flip seats by EOW.
  • Daily three-way reconciliation across one client portfolio. Surface variances same-day.
  • Begin the near-miss log. Every almost-error logged with root cause.
End of Week 2
Daily Cash Rec + Near-Miss Log v1

Five clean days. 5–10 near-miss entries, root-caused.

03
DAYS 15–21
PHASE · CONTROL

Become the control layer.

Be the person who catches things — not the one who needs catching. Solo on a defined client subset.

  • Own ~12 daily payments solo across one client subset — full approval rights, full accountability.
  • Build the pre-wire checklist for each payment type — written like an engineering spec.
  • Document mgmt fee + waterfall mechanics for the client subset. Where the most expensive errors live.
  • Bring structured input to engineering syncs on what's pattern-match vs. needs human judgment.
End of Week 3
Pre-Wire Checklist · Production-Ready

Written like a spec. Steps 1, 3, 4, 5, 8 are pure pattern-match — ship-as-feature candidates.

04
DAYS 22–30
PHASE · IMPROVE

Systematize, then propose.

Hand leadership a credible point of view on what changes in the next 60 days — backed by 4 weeks of evidence.

  • Migrate one approval workflow Slack/email → platform. The JD calls this out specifically.
  • Synthesize 4 weeks of near-miss log into top-5 automation memo, ranked by frequency × severity / lift.
  • Commit to one measurable target for the next 30 days, signed off with leadership.
  • 1:1 with Chris on where I see the next quarter of investment going.
End of Week 4
Top 5 Automation Memo + Workflow Migrated

Tied to existing Hanover Park product surfaces. One workflow live.
